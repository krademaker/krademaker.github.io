**Abstract**

Spinal cord injury represents a huge unmet and lifelong clinical challenge for which no pathology-modifying therapeutics exist to date. Crucially, a lack of single-cell spatial resolution mapping of spinal cord injury hinders our understanding of disease progression and limits the discovery of cell states and pathways that mediate injury and repair. Here we present a spatial transcriptomic atlas of spinal cord injury from pre-injury to late-stage recovery in mice, using ligation and hemisection models together with spatially resolved transcriptomics and single-nucleus RNA-seq. We reveal broad, time-dependent changes in tissue structure, endothelial cells, astrocytes, fibroblasts, and microglia/macrophages, consistent with evolving axon growth and regenerative programs. Through cell-cell communication and trajectory analyses, we uncover dynamic, validated signaling links between proliferating reactive astrocytes and infiltrating immune cells, and identify the key role of endothelial cells in orchestrating the injury response. Our work defines a detailed spatiotemporal atlas of spinal cord injury and provides a foundation for discovering mechanisms that limit recovery and for designing therapies.
